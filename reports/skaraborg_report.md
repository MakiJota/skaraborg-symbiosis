# Regional industrial symbiosis in Skaraborg

*How much of the region's industrial waste could replace raw materials that its firms buy? Reference year 2022.*

This report quantifies the potential for **regional industrial symbiosis** in
Skaraborg's 15 kommuner. Symbiosis here means waste from one firm replacing a
raw or semi-finished material that another firm would otherwise import. The
analysis follows one reproducible method on three nested scales:

- **the region** as a single unit
- **each kommun**, and exchanges between kommuner
- **individual firms**

It also places Skaraborg within Västra Götaland County, analysed with the
same method.

All figures are model estimates built from official statistics. They are not
measured flows. The results are aggregated so that no figure describes a
single firm.

---

## 1. Summary

- **Industry in Skaraborg generates about 0.81 Mt of waste a year** (95 %
  interval 0.59–1.03 Mt):
  - construction 280 kt
  - manufacturing 280 kt
  - water and waste services 150 kt
  - agriculture and forestry 53 kt
  - energy 37 kt
  - quarries 10 kt
- **Production firms import about 0.46 Mt of raw and semi-finished goods a
  year, worth 2.5 billion SEK.** Two-thirds of these imports are minerals (CN
  25) and stone products (CN 68), and the cement and mineral-wool industry
  alone accounts for 62 % of the tonnes. These imports are the demand that
  local waste can replace.
- **How much can be replaced depends above all on what we know about which
  waste can substitute which product.** Each line below is the whole region
  pooled (LP optimum):

  | Matching knowledge | Waste reused | Imports replaced | Value |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Documented industrial exchanges only (MAESTRI) | **20 kt** | **4.3 %** of tonnes | **58 MSEK** |
  | Adding the reviewed waste-to-resource knowledge graph (W2RKG) | **203 kt** | **44 %** of tonnes | **460 MSEK** a year (18 % of the import bill) |

  The first line is the conservative baseline; the second is the
  best-supported broad estimate. Tonnes come from allocations that maximise
  mass and values from allocations that maximise value (§5).
- **The binding constraint is matching knowledge, not demand.** With
  documented exchanges only, 661 of the 810 kt of waste has no known
  compatible product. With the knowledge graph, the main limit becomes
  demand: much of the waste could be used, but the region does not import
  enough of the products it could replace.
- **Kommuner cannot do it alone.** Within kommun borders only 31 % (MAESTRI)
  to 64 % (with W2RKG) of the regional potential is realised. Exchange
  between kommuner closes almost all of the gap: the two-stage kommun
  allocation reaches 98–100 % of the regional optimum.
- **Three hubs receive most exchanged material:**
  - Skövde (cement)
  - Mariestad (paper)
  - Götene (food and stone)

  Lidköping, Falköping and Skara are the largest net suppliers.
- **At firm level**, 12–16 kt (MAESTRI) or 103–121 kt (with W2RKG) can be
  matched firm to firm. The average straight-line distance is 20–26 km.
- **Against the region's total material use** (8.6 Mt, including domestic
  extraction and freight from the rest of Sweden), reused waste could replace
  0.4 % (MAESTRI) to 3.8 % (with W2RKG) of use.
- **Inside Västra Götaland**, Skaraborg is a net receiver of by-products: see
  §9.

## 3. Method

The method has five steps, and all of them can be rerun from the code.

1. **Waste generation (W).** Workers in each kommun and NACE4 class are
   multiplied by Swedish waste-per-worker coefficients. These come from a
   regression of national waste on employment, 2004–2022. The result is split
   into List of Waste (LoW) codes with sector waste profiles.
   - Manufacturing coefficients are scaled to each sub-sector so that the
     national total is kept.
   - Quarries get their own coefficient (100 t per worker), separate from
     metal mining.
2. **Input demand (D).** Imports of each organisation are shared out to its
   workplaces in the region by their share of the organisation's Swedish
   workforce. Only raw and semi-finished goods bought by production sectors
   (A–F) count as demand.
3. **Matching.** A compatibility matrix lists which LoW waste codes can
   replace which CN4 products.
   - **Baseline:** documented industrial exchanges (MAESTRI).
   - **Broad estimate:** MAESTRI plus the W2RKG knowledge graph. The graph
     summarises 33,679 waste-to-resource links from 7,841 scientific papers.
     Only pairs accepted in a plausibility review are used (72 pairs).
   - Implausible pairs are excluded throughout, for example quarry rock as
     coal, ash as refined rare metals, or rubble as gypsum.
4. **Allocation**, on three nested scales:
   - **Region:** Skaraborg as one pool. The linear-programming optimum is the
     maximum that can be reused.
   - **Kommun:** first within each kommun, then between kommuner (two-stage).
   - **Firms:** each firm's waste goes to the nearest compatible firms, on two
     bases. *Bottom-up* uses registry firms only; *top-down* scales the firms
     up to the kommun totals.

   Each scale is run twice: maximising tonnes reused (*mass* regime) or the
   value of replaced imports (*value* regime).
5. **Valuation and indicators.** Replaced tonnes are valued at the unit price
   that the region's own importers pay for each product. The indicators are:
   - share of waste reused
   - share of imported inputs replaced, by mass and by value
   - value replaced
   - the split between intra-kommun and inter-kommun flows
   - distance at firm level

A regional material balance places the results in a wider frame. It combines
domestic extraction, imports and lorry freight in and out, giving apparent
regional material use.

Share of the region's waste reused at the regional optimum: 2.4 % (MAESTRI)
and 25.0 % (MAESTRI + W2RKG).

Readings:

- **Local first, then regional.**
  - Exchanges inside a kommun capture only 31 % (MAESTRI) or 64 % (W2RKG) of
    the tonnes the region could reuse.
  - In value the local share is higher: 67 % and 61 %. High-value streams
    such as food residues for feed, paper and pulp often find users in the
    same kommun. Bulky minerals have to travel.
  - Allowing exchange between kommuner brings the result to within 0–3 % of
    the regional optimum.
- **Firm level is lower** because it uses only registry firms. The registry
  covers 84 % of workers in sectors A–E but has no construction firms, and no
  firm may supply itself. The top-down basis restores the kommun totals of
  waste and demand.

- **Receivers.** Götene, Mariestad and Skövde receive more than they send.
  Their mineral, paper and cement industries draw by-products from across the

- **Suppliers.** Lidköping, Falköping and Skara are the largest net

  regional coordination matters most for them.

  within its own borders. In most kommuner, local reuse is below 10 % of the
  waste.

With documented exchanges only, the same pattern holds at a much smaller
scale (19.5 kt). Mariestad, Götene and Skövde are the receivers; Lidköping and
Falköping supply.

## 8. Against total regional material use

Imports are only 1.5 of the 8.6 Mt the region uses. The rest is domestic
extraction and freight from the rest of Sweden. Allocating waste against
**apparent use** instead of imports gives:

| Matching | Waste reused | Share of regional use (t) | Share of regional use (value) | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MAESTRI | 31 kt | 0.4 % | 0.3 % | 50 MSEK |
| MAESTRI + W2RKG | 305 kt | 3.8 % | 3.8 % | 630 MSEK |
| MAESTRI + W2RKG, without quarry waste | 296 kt | 3.7 % | 3.7 % | 625 MSEK |

Measured against total use, by-products could cover a few percent of
Skaraborg's material needs. The volume is dominated by recycled aggregates
replacing locally quarried stone.

## 9. Skaraborg in Västra Götaland County

Skaraborg is one of the four kommunalförbund of Västra Götaland County. The
others are Göteborgsregionen, Fyrbodal and Sjuhärad, and together the four
cover the county's 49 kommuner, each counted once. Running the same method on
the whole county shows what Skaraborg gains or loses by exchanging
by-products with the rest of the county. Run on its own, Skaraborg reproduces
exactly the results in this report.

**Table 5. Skaraborg alone and as part of the county (kommun two-stage, mass)**

| | MAESTRI: alone | MAESTRI: in county | MAESTRI + W2RKG: alone | MAESTRI + W2RKG: in county |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skaraborg waste used within Skaraborg, kt | 19.5 | 14.8 | 202.1 | 130.6 |
| Skaraborg waste sent to the rest of the county, kt | — | 3.2 | — | 7.0 |
| Waste from the rest of the county used in Skaraborg, kt | — | 30.8 | — | 78.3 |
| Share of Skaraborg's waste reused | 2.4 % | 2.2 % | 24.9 % | 17.0 % |
| Share of Skaraborg's imported inputs replaced (t) | 4.2 % | 9.9 % | 43.8 % | 45.3 % |

- **Inside the county, Skaraborg is a net receiver of by-products.** Larger
  waste streams from Göteborgsregionen and Fyrbodal reach Skaraborg's
  mineral, paper and food industries. More of Skaraborg's demand is met, but
  less of its own waste is used, because some of it is displaced by waste
  from elsewhere.
- **Skaraborg matches its waste to local demand better than any other
  sub-region.** With the knowledge graph it reuses 25 % of its own waste on
  its own, against 3–6 % in the other sub-regions. That alone is 46 % of the
  whole county's potential.
- **For the county as a whole, borders cost a lot:**
  - kommuner each on their own realise 24 % (MAESTRI) or 54 % (with W2RKG)
    of the county's optimum
  - the four kommunalförbund each on their own realise 58 % or 85 %
  - exchange across the whole county is needed for the rest

  County optimum: 141 kt (MAESTRI) or 440 kt (MAESTRI + W2RKG).

Distances and transport costs are not yet part of the allocation. Exchanges
across the county are longer (35–70 km on average at firm level, against
20–26 km within Skaraborg), so some of the county-wide gain may not pay for
its transport, especially for low-value bulk minerals.

The uncertainties, in order of size:

1. **Matching knowledge.** Results range from 20 to 350 kt, depending on
   which waste-to-product pairs are accepted.
2. **Quarry waste per worker.** Swedish statistics do not separate quarries
   from metal mines. The plausible range is 5–1,100 t per worker, and the top
   of that range adds about 80 kt of mineral waste with a market.
3. **Plausibility exclusions.** These change value more than tonnes.
4. **Waste model and allocation rule.** Both are small effects.

### Validation

- **National totals.** Scaled back to Sweden, the waste model reproduces
  Eurostat totals:
  - agriculture 0.69×
  - mining 0.89×
  - manufacturing 1.56×
  - energy 0.89×
  - water and waste 0.86×
  - construction 1.13×
- **Quarries.** Modelled quarry waste is 0.3 % of the quarry output implied
  by SGU statistics, consistent with quarries reporting little unsold
  residue. The former single mining coefficient implied 30 %, which was
  implausible.
- **Prices.** Regional import prices are close to Eurostat unit values for
  the same codes: plastics 0.95×, glass 0.99×, recovered paper 1.39×.
- **Totals add up.** Summing kommun results reproduces the regional totals
  exactly. The top-down firm basis reproduces the kommun totals.

## 11. Limitations

- **Demand covers imports only.** Materials bought from Swedish suppliers
  appear only in the material balance (§8), which has no buyer detail.
- **One tonne for one tonne.** Substitution assumes 1 t of waste replaces
  1 t of product. For conversion routes, such as residues to food
  ingredients, this overstates value.
- **No transport, processing or quality costs.** All values are the gross
  value of replaced imports.
- **Waste is modelled from employment.** Differences between kommuner
  reflect industry mix, not the performance of individual plants.
- **No construction firms in the firm registry.** Construction waste enters
  the firm level only on the top-down basis.
- **Planned change at the largest buyer.** The Skövde cement plant plans to
  stop clinker production from 2027, which would reduce the largest single
  demand for mineral and fuel substitutes.

## 12. Reproducibility and data availability

The analysis is a scripted R pipeline. The same code runs for Skaraborg, each
of the other kommunalförbund and the whole county, by changing one setting
(the study area). Microdata from SCB are confidential and are not shared.
The interactive web report that accompanies this document shows aggregated
results only:

- industries with fewer than three organisations in a kommun are merged
- imported products with fewer than three importers in a cell are merged
- no firm-level locations or links are published
