How the Skaraborg estimates are made

The study follows a reproducible pipeline for the 15 kommuner in Skaraborg, using 2022 as its reference year. The county-wide Västra Götaland study applies the same core method.

  1. Estimate waste generation. Workplace employment by kommun and industry is multiplied by Swedish waste-per-worker coefficients estimated from national waste and employment data. Waste is split into List of Waste codes using sector profiles; quarry waste is estimated separately.
  2. Estimate input demand. SCB import data for raw and semi-finished goods in production sectors are assigned to local workplaces by their share of the organisation's Swedish workforce. Purchases from Swedish suppliers are not visible as firm-level demand in these data.
  3. Match waste to products. A compatibility matrix connects waste codes to four-digit imported products. The baseline uses documented exchanges from MAESTRI; broader scenarios add plausible, reviewed links from the W2RKG knowledge graph.
  4. Allocate compatible flows. The model allocates waste to compatible demand for the region as a whole, within and between kommuner, and between anonymised firms. The regional result includes an optimisation benchmark; kommun allocation prioritises local matches; firm allocation prioritises nearby compatible buyers.
  5. Value and compare. Allocated tonnes are valued at estimated regional import unit prices. Separate mass- and value-prioritising runs and alternative matching assumptions show how sensitive results are.

Limitations

  • One tonne of waste is assumed to replace one tonne of product, although real processing can require more material or produce losses.
  • Quarry-waste intensities and waste-to-product compatibility are uncertain; scenario results should be read as a range.
  • Allocation rules rank theoretical compatible flows. They do not prove technical suitability, commercial demand, logistics, contracts or actual exchanges.
